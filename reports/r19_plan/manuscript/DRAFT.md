# Matched drug-response measurements improve hepatic perturbation prediction within L1000

Development manuscript. Intended scope and outstanding evidence are recorded in the [claims audit](CLAIMS.md).

## Abstract

We evaluated hepatic perturbation prediction under separate vehicle roles and held-out chemical scaffolds. In adult hepatocellular carcinoma cells at 10 µM, calibrating the mean of eight same-drug nonhepatic measurements by donor response amplitude reduced root mean square error (RMSE) by 1.720% in JHH-5 and 2.609% in JHH-7 versus a constant gain. With the same gain specification, gene-wise shrinkage added 0.014% and 0.071%, respectively, and both 99% intervals included zero. In HEPG2, a scaffold-blocked rank-16 operator reduced 24-hour response RMSE from measured 6-hour inputs by 4.164% versus a centered scalar and by 3.382% versus diagonal ridge (99% intervals 2.709–5.656% and 2.081–4.731%). The locked R18 kernel–FiLM evaluation missed its primary joint criterion. Between-line contrasts and lower-dose or cross-assay transfer exposed limits to generalization. Independent adult HCC culture validation remains pending.

## Introduction

Transcriptomic measurements can connect gene-expression changes across drugs and biological contexts. Predicting a response before the experiment requires a precise specification of measurements available at inference. A molecular structure, a measured response in another cell line, and a measurement at an earlier time give access to different information. Their performance can also depend on whether vehicle variation is shared between model fitting and evaluation. We distinguish these input conditions and test time, cell context and architecture changes under independent vehicle roles [1]. The comparisons address four questions about vehicle independence, measured same-drug inputs, fair calibration, and transfer across biological and assay contexts.

## Results

### How do shared controls affect model selection and model ranking?

With the physical vehicle sample counts held equal between protocols, separating the fitting and selection roles reduced the selected residual gain in each of the six nonzero-gain comparator families. The relative ranking of the models depended on whether selection used an independent vehicle group. Across the legacy model families, joint selection of the training checkpoint and residual gain also affected the matched score. These comparisons motivated the common fitting, selection and scoring roles used below. [Role and selection comparisons](../D12_RESULTS.md)

### What does measured same-drug RNA contribute?

Two subsequent controls used the original fitted settings and reproduced the frozen predictions for the exact 6-hour donor. For all 132 compounds, RMSE was 7.25% lower than predicting the fitted 24-hour population mean (99% interval 5.26–9.28%). Where a different held-out compound was available at the same dose, the exact compound input lowered RMSE by 16.11% relative to the input substitution (89 parents, 99% interval 12.48–19.92%). These controls quantify the contribution of measured compound-specific response in the same assay. [Input attribution](../T4_ATTRIBUTION_RESULTS.md)

The donor mean also carried a compound-specific signal across cell lines. In JHH-5 at 10 µM, replacing the drug’s nonhepatic donor measurements with a different drug from the same plate, fold and dose increased RMSE by the equivalent of a 7.60% gain for matched inputs (99% interval 6.04–9.29%). The off-position eight-donor experiments retained this pattern after subtracting the response mean in each evaluation pool. With centered contrasts, top-1 retrieval was 24.37% in JHH-5 and 40.53% in JHH-7, compared with chance of 5.83% and 3.41%. Both retrieval intervals against the best matched public adaptation included zero. Figure 2 gives the donor count and comparator sensitivity. [Donor attribution](../JHH5_DONOR_ATTRIBUTION_RESULTS.md) · [Centered contrasts](../JHH_OFF_POSITION_CONTRAST_RESULTS.md)

### When do complex models add value beyond strong simple calibration?

We applied the same amplitude-dependent gain specification to the raw donor mean and to the shrunk donor mean, fitting their coefficients separately on the original training splits. The adaptive raw mean reduced RMSE over the fixed scalar by 1.720% (99% interval 1.085–2.414%) in JHH-5 and 2.609% (0.456–4.996%) in JHH-7. Under matched amplitude calibration, shrinkage reduced RMSE by 0.014% (−0.050 to 0.084%) and 0.071% (−0.094 to 0.228%), respectively. The paired 99% intervals also excluded pooled R² improvements of 0.5 percentage points in both lines. Drug-specific amplitude calibration explained the practically relevant improvement on these cohorts. Figure 3 and Table 1 show the matched comparisons. [Matched amplitude comparison](../JHH_DONOR_AMPLITUDE_FAIR_RESULTS.md)

In the 132-parent scaffold-blocked HEPG2 benchmark, rank-16 achieved RMSE 0.90779 and pooled R² 25.930%. The earlier origin-scalar comparison showed a 7.24% RMSE improvement. Against the centered scalar, the operator reduced RMSE by 4.164% (paired 99% interval 2.709–5.656%) and improved pooled R² by 5.428 points (2.984–7.918). Against gene-wise diagonal ridge, its RMSE reduction was 3.382% (2.081–4.731%) and its pooled R² increase was 4.320 points (2.116–6.590). With matched means, measured inputs and eligible fits, the two tests support incremental value for cross-gene time mapping in HEPG2 (Fig. 1 and Table 1). [Mean-shift controls](../T4_MEAN_SHIFT_RESULTS.md)

A matched State Transition adaptation [2] received one token containing the same 6-hour vector and a shared intervention code. Rank-16 reduced RMSE by 7.44% (99% interval 5.33–9.54%) and increased pooled R² by 15.27 points (11.99–18.32). The direct different-plate 24-hour repeat was more accurate when calibrated on independent fitting observations. [Matched State](../T4_STATE_RESULTS.md) · [Calibrated repeat](../T4_REPEAT_RESULTS.md)

The earlier fixed-gain shrinkage result compared State, XPert [3], PRnet [4], chemCPA [5] and TranSiGen [6] adapted to matched measured inputs and restricted L1000 training. Against the best of these adaptations selected per metric in each draw, shrinkage reduced RMSE by 0.751% in JHH-5 and 1.278% in JHH-7. Three residual adapters chose zero gain in all rotations. These comparisons preceded the fair calibration test and concern specific refits on 305 and 88 B1-exposed compounds. [JHH-5 matched adaptations](../JHH5_OFF_POSITION_NATIVE_RESULTS.md) · [JHH-7 matched adaptations](../JHH7_OFF_POSITION_NATIVE_RESULTS.md)

On the previously scored HEPG2 24-hour C1 cohort, R18 passed 4 of 12 registered comparisons with structure inputs (502 parents) and 13 of 14 with same-drug nonhepatic measurements (500 parents). With measured inputs, RMSE was 0.02024 lower than the best reference (99% interval 0.01731–0.02329), and the pooled R² gain over the R15 kernel was +0.183 points (−1.419 to +1.983). The C1 joint confirmation criterion was unmet in both information settings (Table 1 and Fig. 1). [R18 results](../R18_RESULTS.md)

The remaining head, loss, basal-context and phenotype-view comparisons are summarized with their matched controls and failure criteria in Supplementary Table S1. [Supplementary Table S1](SUPPLEMENTARY_TABLE_S1.md)

### How far do effects transfer across cell lines, doses and assays?

We preregistered a secondary analysis in primary human hepatocytes after observing the HEPG2 results. The within-context analysis used 67 held-out parents and 105 independent later wells across 11 RNA plates. The scalar reduced RMSE by 1.52% relative to zero response (99% interval 0.82–2.25%) and increased pooled R² by 3.72 percentage points (2.14–5.45). For the fixed-rank operator relative to the scalar, RMSE gain was −0.97% (−4.47 to 2.26%) and the pooled R² change was −0.39 percentage points (−7.62 to 5.63). This comparison used a rank of 16 and ridge penalty of 1, fixed before scoring, with separately fitted PHH coefficients. [Primary human hepatocyte analysis](../T4_PHH_RESULTS.md)

Applying the HEPG2 rank-16 operator to primary human hepatocytes increased RMSE by 4.54% relative to the HEPG2 scalar and 6.18% relative to the PHH scalar. Removing the HEPG2 expression intercept eliminated 0.04275 of the 0.04832 excess RMSE over the HEPG2 scalar. A scalar-anchored low-rank residual still increased error. [PHH transfer](../T4_CONTEXT_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md) · [Transfer ablations](../T4_CONTEXT_RESIDUAL_RESULTS.md)

Exact concentration and parent matching yielded five compounds and 21 physical wells in GSE284321 and three compounds and nine wells in GSE296254. On their corresponding independent L1000 observations, the 6-to-24-hour prediction had parent-macro cosine of 0.247 and 0.220. On the identical 827-gene axis in the respective HepaRG datasets, cosine was 0.024 and −0.017. Direct 24-hour L1000 donors also had near-zero mean cosine in HepaRG. The two comparisons jointly change assay and cell context. Their small supports limit population inference. [Matched cross-assay comparison](../T4_CROSS_ASSAY_RESULTS.md)

We applied donor-amplitude calibration fitted exclusively at 10 µM to JHH-5 measurements at 3.33333 and 1.11111 µM, with eight off-position nonhepatic donor responses at the exact evaluation dose. The amplitude gain reduced RMSE by 1.195% and 1.287% versus a constant gain fitted at 10 µM. Its RMSE was 0.232% and 0.591% higher than zero response on the 111 and 114 scoring parents, respectively. Both low-dose comparisons missed the joint criterion. The two cohorts used separate treatment wells on five shared plates. [Position-disjoint dose transfer](../JHH_NORM_GAIN_DOSE_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md)

For 86 drugs measured in both adult HCC lines, we evaluated the JHH-7 minus JHH-5 response after centering the matched difference within parent fold and vehicle role. Each line contributed one target plate with corresponding RNA well indices. The observed difference had a vector RMS of 0.7315, and the candidate difference had an RMS of 0.0233. Its RMSE exceeded that of a zero difference by 0.130% (99% interval 0.010–0.265%), with a pooled R² change of −0.324 percentage points (−0.688 to −0.004). Restricting the comparison to 84 identical nonhepatic source and vehicle sets retained the result. Cell context and target plate were confounded in this contrast. [Matched between-cell differences](../JHH_OFF_POSITION_INTERACTION_RESULTS.md)

In R18 C2, measured-input kernel–FiLM passed all comparisons at two of nine supported concentrations, 0.0411523 and 0.123457 µM. C3 provided descriptive HUH7 comparisons on 12 and 14 parents. Its HEPG2-only fit led the best matched reference at 6 and 24 hours and did not meet the joint criterion against zero response. In the separate HepaRG development analysis, independent within-assay repeats reduced RMSE by 5.90% where available, and a nonhepatic LINCS donor map increased RMSE by 0.71% against the frozen same-assay reference on the 175 covered parents. [R18 dose and context](../R18_RESULTS.md) · [HepaRG repeated wells](../HEPARG_POSITION_REPEAT_RESULTS.md) · [HepaRG donor comparison](../HEPARG_DONOR_RESULTS.md)

## Methods

### Physical units and information available at inference

LINCS samples were indexed by release, cell type, canonical compound parent and RNA plate–well coordinate. We required measured concentrations and validated exact dose–time pairs against original instance and signature-quality metadata for the temporal comparison. The model using structure and the model supplied with actual same-compound expression were evaluated separately. In the matched nonhepatic transfer comparison, each donor cell line contributed its mean fit-vehicle contrast, and the four cell lines contributed equally. For validation and scoring, independent vehicle wells formed the reference contrast. Physical wells were kept together across reuse of the same well and rotations of the three vehicle roles. Data reserved for the separately registered confirmation study were excluded from development inputs. [Temporal protocol](../T4_PROTOCOL.md) · [Matched donor protocol](../SL_B_PROTOCOL.md)

### Adult HCC donor transfer

We selected well-level CMap 2020 measurements at exactly 10 µM and 24 hours with published instance QC and a resolved compound structure. The JHH-5 and JHH-7 target wells were part of a prior project development inventory. Source measurements were limited to annotated nonhepatic cell lines, with at least three eligible vehicle wells per cell–RNA plate context. Within each vehicle rotation, source responses were calculated against the fit vehicle group. We averaged treated wells within each source cell and RNA plate, then gave equal weight to plates within a cell and to distinct cells within a chemical parent. Targets used a disjoint vehicle group. We excluded target drug labels, physical plates and Bemis–Murcko scaffolds from comparator fitting. For the JHH-7 comparison, all comparator fitting labels came from JHH-5. [JHH-5 contract](../HCC_LINCS_24H_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md) · [JHH-7 contract](../JHH7_DONOR_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md)

The fit-free estimator attenuated each donor mean using its squared value divided by the sum of its squared value and the cross-cell sample variance divided by the number of donor cells. We assigned zero when that denominator was zero. Cross-cell variance describes disagreement among measured contexts. In the panel-size comparison we fixed eight nested hash-based orders of eligible donors for every parent. All target wells and vehicle rotations of a parent received the same orders. We evaluated each panel's prediction against the same outcome and averaged the resulting losses. [Shrinkage and attribution](../JHH5_DONOR_ATTRIBUTION_PROTOCOL.md) · [Donor subsampling](../HCC_DONOR_PANEL_SIZE_PROTOCOL.md)

In the matched eight-donor comparison, the fixed State Transition architecture took the corresponding donor mean as a single RNA token. The training schedule cycled through the eight panels for each fitting parent, with five visits to each panel in 40 epochs. The origin scalar was fitted on the same JHH-5 training contrasts using all eight panel inputs at equal weight. All comparator predictions were frozen before score-vehicle and target measurements were read. [Matched eight-donor contract](../HCC_DONOR8_STATE_PROTOCOL.md)

We registered a separate exact-dose comparison at 3.33333 µM using the same models, panel draws, gene axis and scoring rules. Its 495 JHH-5 treatment wells had no overlap with the previously evaluated treatment-well IDs. The evaluation shared five RNA plates, all 65 eligible target vehicle wells and 456 compound parents with the 10 µM comparison. Within the low-dose analysis, the scoring plate, parent fold and its scaffolds were excluded from the fit, and donor RNA came from other plates and nonhepatic cell lines. [Low-dose support and prior exposure](../JHH_LOW_DOSE_SUPPORT.md) · [Low-dose contract](../JHH_LOW_DOSE_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md)

A separate dose-bridge comparison predicted 1.11111 µM JHH-5 responses from nonhepatic measurements of the same compound at 1.11111 and 10 µM. Each source context contained both doses for the same Broad compound identifier, cell line and RNA plate, with shared eligible vehicle wells. The target comprised 472 previously unscored treatment wells from 467 compounds. The prediction used two constrained, gene-independent coefficients fitted under the same held parent, scaffold and plate exclusions. Each source alone had a fitted scalar comparator, and a State adaptation received the two dose-specific vectors as separate tokens. A within-evaluation-pool substitution replaced only the high-dose vector with a response to another compound. [Dose-bridge contract](../JHH_DOSE_BRIDGE_PROTOCOL.md)

For the low-dose transfer to JHH-7, we fitted the scalar and the one-token State adaptation only on JHH-5 at 1.11111 µM. We excluded JHH-7 evaluation parents and scaffolds from both fits. Each evaluation prediction used nonhepatic data matched to its JHH-7 concentration. The JHH-7 1 and 0.1 µM measurements were reserved for scoring and analyzed separately on the single available target RNA plate. The wrong-compound control used the fitted scalar and a donor vector from the same evaluation concentration. [JHH-7 low-dose contract](../JHH7_LOW_DOSE_PROTOCOL.md)

For the position-disjoint JHH-5 analysis at 10 µM, we removed every donor well whose RNA row and column matched any target well for the same compound. We selected eight donor cell lines by a fixed hash order. The eight-token State adaptation received all eight donor vectors, and the calibrated shrinkage and scalar models received the same measurements. Each model was fitted with the same held parent, scaffold and physical plate exclusions. A within-evaluation-pool permutation provided wrong-compound controls with the corresponding fitting gain. [Off-position eight-source contract](../JHH5_OFF_POSITION_PROTOCOL.md)

We transferred the same 10 µM recipe to JHH-7. Both scalar gains and the eight-token State comparator used only JHH-5 labels at 10 µM after excluding the chemical parents and scaffolds of the full registered JHH-7 10 µM cohort. The score subset consisted of JHH-7 parents with eight admissible off-position nonhepatic sources. The JHH-7 outcome plate and the fitting plates were physically disjoint. [Cross-cell off-position contract](../JHH7_OFF_POSITION_PROTOCOL.md)

The four added public models received a pseudobasal vector formed from a JHH-5 fit-role mean vehicle and the fitted donor scalar. Each model also received its registered molecular features. Model weights were initialized afresh, and the same 203 JHH-5 compound groups supplied the target contrasts. We used an internal split of 163 fitting and 40 selection parents and disjoint vehicles to select epochs and residual gains, then refit on the full training set. On JHH-5 the same procedure used 96–148 internal fitting and 24–36 selection parents, with 122–183 parents available for each refit after excluding the score plate, parent fold and scaffolds. The best public reference was selected separately for RMSE and pooled R² in each paired resample. [JHH-5 contract](../JHH5_OFF_POSITION_NATIVE_PROTOCOL.md) · [JHH-7 contract](../JHH7_OFF_POSITION_NATIVE_PROTOCOL.md)

### Temporal model and comparators

The 6-hour vector was centered with the source mean and projected onto principal directions learned only from fitting conditions. A ridge map estimated the 24-hour vector from those coordinates, and the fitting mean was added to the output. Each parent had equal total fitting weight. Rank (4, 16 or 32) and ridge penalty (0.1 or 1) were selected from internally held parents and disjoint vehicle means. The early identity vector, a source-fitted nonnegative scalar and a different-plate 24-hour donor provided comparators. The scaffold-blocked follow-up fixed rank 16 and ridge 1. The centered scalar used fit-only means and an unrestricted common slope. The diagonal ridge fit an independent slope for each gene and selected its shared penalty by leave-one-source-parent-out squared error. The fit-weighting, query inputs and split registry were shared by all arms. Exact-condition controls retained the same trained operator. For transfer to HepaRG we used the registered one-to-one Entrez–Ensembl panel, preserved the frozen L1000 predictions, and computed cosine against independent plate vehicle subtractions in TempO-Seq. [Time model and scoring contract](../T4_PROTOCOL.md) · [Cross-assay protocol](../T4_HEPARG_PROTOCOL.md)

### R18 confirmation

R18 refitted the registered R17 candidate and comparators for each cohort with the same three vehicle roles, parent folds and selection rules. All 108 prediction slices were frozen together before scoring on the disjoint vehicle contrasts. C1 used the previously evaluated 5–20 µM HEPG2 wells. C2 retained measured concentrations as distinct strata. C3 compared HEPG2-only fitting and fitting with additional eligible HUH7 parents. C1 required improvement in every prespecified comparison on both endpoints at 99% confidence. [R18 protocol](../R18_CONFIRMATION_PROTOCOL.md) · [Execution and verification](../R18_EXECUTION.md)

### Estimands and uncertainty

Physical-well root mean square errors were averaged within parent, then across parents. Pooled R² used the total squared error relative to a zero treatment contrast. Paired intervals used the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles of 10,000 resamples of whole chemical parents with their wells and vehicle rotations. The matched bootstrap intervals summarize effects at the registered support. Primary comparisons across four development axes used one Holm family of one-sided paired randomization tests at a family-wise alpha of 0.01, retaining the registered A and B slots. The analysis of the small exact HepaRG cohorts used parent-level cosine and observed ranges. [Primary-test registry and adjustment](../data/dev_family/primary.tsv)

## Discussion

The matched-control designs distinguish signal supplied by the measured drug from improvements due to the learned mapping. In adult JHH lines, predictive gains mainly followed the calibration of the measured donor amplitude. In HEPG2, cross-gene time mapping added predictive value beyond fitting the intercept and per-gene slopes. The comparison with calibrated 24-hour repeats estimates the accuracy available from another later plate.

The existing cross-platform comparisons are narrow. Exact chemical and concentration matching substantially reduces support. Differences between HEPG2 and HepaRG and between expression assays occur together. The observations cannot isolate a platform effect from a cell-context effect. HEPG2 is hepatoblastoma-derived [7], so even the same-assay result does not establish drug-response accuracy in adult hepatocellular carcinoma. A separate evaluation needs independent tumour-cell contexts, randomized physical positions, matched time and exposure, independent vehicle roles and the best available comparator under the same inputs.

BioPert [8] uses same-drug transcriptomic representations measured in a reference cell line for cross-context prediction. Our adult HCC analysis compares multiple off-position nonhepatic measurements at the same exposure, holding their inputs fixed while varying the calibration and mapping. Systema [9] isolates perturbation-specific response from systematic variation in genetic perturbation prediction. Our attribution and centered-pool scores use different-drug substitutions within the measured chemical design. Nicol et al. [10] show how using the same controls to construct predicted and observed expression shifts can inflate correlations. We evaluated how equal-sized, disjoint vehicle roles change model and gain selection, then kept physical reference roles separate in the matched comparisons.

The remaining independent evidence is limited to the registered pilot design and archived forecasts. Clinical or cross-assay generalization requires resolved age and cell lineage, randomized well positions, independent cultures, and matched dose and time. Several exploratory SNU-423 comparisons were sensitive to chemical similarity and plate position. [Exploratory scope and outcomes](SUPPLEMENTARY_TABLE_S1.md)
