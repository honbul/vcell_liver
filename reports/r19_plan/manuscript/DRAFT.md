# Matched drug-response measurements improve hepatic perturbation prediction within L1000

Development manuscript. Intended scope and outstanding evidence are recorded in the [claims audit](CLAIMS.md).

## Abstract

We tested whether measured same-drug responses improved hepatic perturbation prediction under disjoint vehicle roles and held-out chemical parents. In HEPG2 L1000, a low-rank model predicted independent 24-hour wells from 6-hour measurements for 132 compounds. With scaffold exclusion, RMSE was 7.24% lower than a matched scalar (99% interval 5.09–9.38%). In adult JHH-5 hepatocellular carcinoma cells, calibrated shrinkage of eight same-drug measurements from different RNA well indices reduced RMSE by 0.751% versus the best of five public model adaptations (99% interval 0.558–0.974%, 305 previously studied compounds at 10 µM across six evaluation plates). At 3.33333 and 1.11111 µM in JHH-5, a same-dose scalar had lower error than gene-wise shrinkage and two-dose blending, respectively. The same 10 µM recipe, fitted only on chemically disjoint JHH-5 parents, reduced RMSE by 1.278% versus the resampled best of five public adaptations (0.680–2.005%) on 88 JHH-7 compounds from one previously studied plate. At 1 µM, a JHH-5-fitted scalar reduced RMSE by 2.68% versus zero response on the JHH-7 plate. The results support measured same-drug inputs within L1000. Independent adult HCC evaluation across assay batches remains unresolved.

## Introduction

Transcriptomic measurements can connect gene-expression changes across drugs and biological contexts. Predicting a response before the experiment requires a precise specification of measurements available at inference. A molecular structure, a measured response in another cell line, and a measurement at an earlier time give access to different information. Their performance can also depend on whether vehicle variation is shared between model fitting and evaluation. We distinguish these input conditions and test time, cell context and architecture changes under independent vehicle roles. [L1000 resource study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.10.049)

## Results

### Control independence changed the model comparison

With the physical vehicle sample counts held equal between protocols, separating the fitting and selection roles reduced the selected residual gain in each of the six nonzero-gain comparator families. The relative ranking of the models depended on whether selection used an independent vehicle group. Across the legacy model families, joint selection of the training checkpoint and residual gain also affected the matched score. These comparisons motivated the common fitting, selection and scoring roles used below. [Role and selection comparisons](../D12_RESULTS.md)

### A paired temporal model improved within-assay prediction

Of the exact-concentration matched HEPG2 conditions, 247 across 132 chemical parents had independent RNA plates at both 6 and 24 hours and at least two RNA plates at each time. We assigned one 24-hour scoring plate to each condition and excluded the entire plate from fitting, selection and direct 24-hour donor aggregation in that comparison. On 268 physical scoring wells, the temporal model lowered parent-macro RMSE from 0.97896 for the scalar model to 0.90722 and increased pooled R² from 11.64% to 25.96%. The paired R² change was 14.32 percentage points (99% interval 10.42–18.06). The comparison with a direct measurement on a different 24-hour plate included zero in both intervals. [Temporal analysis](../T4_RESULTS.md)

Two subsequent controls used the original fitted settings and reproduced the frozen predictions for the exact 6-hour donor. For all 132 compounds, RMSE was 7.25% lower than predicting the fitted 24-hour population mean (99% interval 5.26–9.28%). Where a different held-out compound was available at the same dose, the exact compound input lowered RMSE by 16.11% relative to the input substitution (89 parents, 99% interval 12.48–19.92%). These controls quantify the contribution of measured compound-specific response in the same assay. [Input attribution](../T4_ATTRIBUTION_RESULTS.md)

In a post hoc influence analysis, the temporal model reduced RMSE relative to the scalar comparator in 95 of 132 parents. Removing the 20 largest positive parent-level RMSE contributions left a 3.95% relative RMSE gain. Independently reweighting parents and physical plates gave relative RMSE gains of 5.05–9.75% across the central 99% of weighted comparisons. The different-plate 24-hour donor comparison again included zero. [Parent and plate sensitivity](../ROBUSTNESS_RESULTS.md)

We subsequently refitted the fixed rank-16 operator after excluding all source parents with a Bemis–Murcko scaffold found in the evaluation fold. On the same 132 held parents, the operator reduced RMSE by 7.24% (99% interval 5.09–9.38%) and increased pooled R² by 14.27 percentage points (10.57–17.96) versus a scalar fitted after the same exclusions. A matched-count random deletion resulted in similar point estimates, and the paired intervals between the two operator fits included zero. This comparison held the measured 6-hour inputs and 24-hour outcome wells fixed. [Scaffold-purged time transfer](../T4_SCAFFOLD_RESULTS.md)

In a registered comparison on the same scaffold-purged support, we fitted the published State Transition architecture with one input token containing the measured 978-gene 6-hour contrast per condition and one shared intervention code. The fitted compound and dose pairs were identical to those used by the low-rank operator. The operator reduced RMSE by 7.44% relative to this State adaptation (99% interval 5.33–9.54%) and increased pooled R² by 15.27 percentage points (11.99–18.32). Bootstrap intervals using scaffold clusters supported both gains. The paired comparisons of State with the same-data scalar included zero for both endpoints. [Matched State comparison](../T4_STATE_RESULTS.md)

Restricting measured 6-hour inputs to different RNA well indices supported 59 parents and 82 evaluation wells. On that support the operator reduced RMSE by 6.82% (99% interval 4.25–9.54%) relative to the same-input scalar. Among 35 parents with both position categories, the scalar led under both categories after donor counts were matched. The existing plate assignments limit interpretation of position effects. In a separate frozen rescoring on 69 parents, plate- and dose-centered RMSE was 4.51% lower for the operator than for State (2.42–6.82%). Top-1 compound retrieval was 59.66% for the operator, 48.07% for State and 75.60% for the scalar. [Position and retrieval](../T4_POSITION_RESULTS.md)

We preregistered a secondary analysis in primary human hepatocytes after observing the HEPG2 results. The within-context analysis used 67 held-out parents and 105 independent later wells across 11 RNA plates. The scalar reduced RMSE by 1.52% relative to zero response (99% interval 0.82–2.25%) and increased pooled R² by 3.72 percentage points (2.14–5.45). For the fixed-rank operator relative to the scalar, RMSE gain was −0.97% (−4.47 to 2.26%) and the pooled R² change was −0.39 percentage points (−7.62 to 5.63). This comparison used a rank of 16 and ridge penalty of 1, fixed before scoring, with separately fitted PHH coefficients. [Primary human hepatocyte analysis](../T4_PHH_RESULTS.md)

In a subsequent registered development comparison, we applied a HEPG2-fitted rank-16 temporal operator to the same 6-hour PHH measurements used by the scalar comparators. Source fitting excluded the evaluation parent fold. On the 67 PHH parents, using the operator increased RMSE by 4.54% (99% interval 3.21–5.85%) relative to the HEPG2-fitted scalar and by 6.18% (5.09–7.33%) relative to the PHH-fitted scalar. Pooled R² decreased by 9.80 and 12.11 percentage points, respectively. Source chemistry and plate support varied across the two cell contexts. [Same-assay context transfer](../T4_CONTEXT_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md)

Holding the linear weights fixed while dropping the source-derived gene-wise intercept removed 0.04275 of the 0.04832 excess PHH RMSE over the source scalar. Adding the same intercept to the scalar increased RMSE by 3.59% (99% interval 2.93–4.24%). An intercept-free low-rank residual on top of the source scalar still increased RMSE by 1.83% (1.21–2.55%) relative to the scalar. The reverse PHH-to-HEPG2 comparison also favored the source scalar over the anchored residual. [Transfer ablations](../T4_CONTEXT_RESIDUAL_RESULTS.md)

In a subsequent registered comparison, we calibrated direct 24-hour replicates using independent 24-hour plates of the fitting parents. The 6-hour operator had higher RMSE than the scalar-calibrated 24-hour repeat by 0.04308 in HEPG2 (99% interval 0.02266–0.06476) and 0.04627 in PHH (0.01547–0.07690). Its pooled R² was lower by 6.56 and 8.05 percentage points. The 6-hour operator can help when early measurements are the available input, and a calibrated direct later measurement was more accurate in both matched cohorts. [Calibrated repeat comparison](../T4_REPEAT_RESULTS.md)

In adult JHH-5, we compared same-drug 24-hour transfer at exactly 10 µM in CMap L1000 while jointly holding out evaluation plates and Bemis–Murcko scaffolds. All 609 parents and 615 target wells were in the prior B1 development inventory. The rank-16 residual increased RMSE by 2.07% (99% interval 1.25–2.99%) relative to the equally informed affine calibration, and pooled R² was 4.97 percentage points lower (2.66–7.48). The absolute RMSE interval against one-token State crossed zero, and both intervals against the residual parent shuffle crossed zero. The direct nonhepatic donor mean and its origin scalar gave the best point estimates for pooled R² and RMSE, respectively. The transfer used measurements at the target time, and only six parents had independent-position target repeats. [JHH-5 exact-dose transfer](../HCC_LINCS_24H_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md)

In a subsequent registered development comparison, the frozen donor mean reduced RMSE by 4.04% relative to the equal-input State adaptation (99% interval 2.91–5.31%) and by 7.60% relative to substituting another compound from the same plate, fold and dose (6.04–9.29%). A gene-wise attenuation using variance across donor cell lines and no JHH-5 fit responses reduced RMSE by a further 0.645% versus the donor mean (0.535–0.771%) and increased pooled R² by 0.953 points (0.703–1.276). It also improved on the target-fit origin scalar and had similar retrieval ranks to the donor mean. The improvement was observed in the 605 parents with at least 16 donor lines. Cross-cell variance was used as a weighting statistic rather than as a measure of measurement replication. [JHH-5 donor attribution and shrinkage](../JHH5_DONOR_ATTRIBUTION_RESULTS.md)

On one CMap JHH-7 RNA plate at the same measured dose and time, the direct donor mean reduced RMSE by 13.71% versus a State adaptation trained only on 475 JHH-5 parents after excluding all JHH-7 evaluation parents and scaffolds (99% interval 9.82–17.49%). Mean predictions also improved on the zero and wrong-parent controls. The fixed shrinkage gain over the donor mean was 0.260% (0.055–0.491%), and the pooled R² interval included zero. Ninety-five of the 99 JHH-7 evaluation parents were measured in the JHH-5 development cohort. The single target plate and prior B1 exposure constrain inference to a technical cross-cell comparison. [JHH-7 donor transfer](../JHH7_DONOR_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md)

To measure sensitivity to donor input coverage, we evaluated independently scored nested subsets on 605 JHH-5 and 97 JHH-7 compounds with at least 16 measured donor lines. Reducing the mean from 16 to eight lines increased RMSE by 3.02% in JHH-5 and 3.03% in JHH-7, exceeding the registered 0.5% margin. The fixed shrinkage formula mitigated the loss and retained lower pooled RMSE than zero response for eight donors in both cohorts. Leaving one JHH-5 plate out yielded positive and negative RMSE gain estimates for the eight-line shrinkage comparison against zero response. The smaller panels were drawn from well-supported compounds and may differ from panels available in deployment. [Donor panel size](../HCC_DONOR_PANEL_SIZE_RESULTS.md)

On the same eight-cell panels, shrinkage reduced RMSE by 2.31% (99% interval 1.30–3.50%) in JHH-5 and 10.20% (6.26–14.01%) on the JHH-7 technical plate versus newly fitted State adaptations. Gains over State and the direct mean were positive in all three JHH-5 folds for both endpoints. The JHH-5 RMSE gain versus a scalar fitted on those panels was 0.194% (−0.305–0.780%), and the joint criterion across the three comparators was unmet. [Matched State comparison](../HCC_DONOR8_STATE_RESULTS.md) · [Figure 2](figure2.pdf)

On 490 JHH-5 parents at 3.33333 µM, the RMSE gain over State was 0.13% (99% interval −0.25 to 0.53%). Shrinkage increased RMSE by 1.88% (1.56–2.19%) and decreased pooled R² by 3.24 points (2.45–4.01) relative to the fitted scalar. Both endpoints also favored the zero-response reference. The direct donor mean had a higher error than shrinkage, and the registered joint improvement criterion was unmet. The two dose comparisons used distinct treatment wells. They shared target plates and vehicles. [Low-dose comparison](../JHH_LOW_DOSE_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md) · [Figure 2](figure2.pdf)

On 467 JHH-5 parents at 1.11111 µM, the linear combination of matching low- and high-dose donors reduced RMSE by 2.029% relative to State given the same two sources (99% interval 1.815–2.254%). Its RMSE gain versus the same-dose-only scalar was −0.013% (−0.069 to 0.028%). Pairing the correct compound's 10 µM response reduced RMSE by 0.080% relative to replacing that response with another compound's (0.010–0.139%). The prespecified minimum gain was unmet against the same-dose-only scalar and the compound-substitution control. [Dose-bridge comparison](../JHH_DOSE_BRIDGE_RESULTS.md)

At 1 µM in JHH-7, a scalar fitted only on JHH-5 reduced RMSE by 6.10% versus a matched State adaptation (99% interval 4.86–7.50%), 2.68% versus zero response (1.32–4.28%) and 3.59% versus a gain-matched wrong-compound donor (2.36–4.93%). ### Architecture changes in HEPG2 produced smaller effects

In the matched 10 µM, 6-hour benchmark with 493 parents and 2,324 HEPG2 wells, the full structure-kernel and FiLM reference reduced RMSE from 0.95987 for the donor anchor to 0.94022. The paired absolute RMSE difference was −0.01965 (99% interval −0.02245 to −0.01687), and pooled R² increased by 3.27 percentage points (99% interval 2.71–3.83). A separate fit-only mean residual did not close this gap. Each of six output or loss changes had an upper 99% bound below 0.5% for relative RMSE gain. The low-rank output used fewer dense multiply-accumulate operations and had an RMSE gain of 0.026%. With donor and kernel held fixed, shuffling structure input in the neural branch had similar prediction scores. This control limits claims about the amount of additional chemical identity learned by that branch. [Donor-matched ablations](../SL_B_RESULTS.md)

On the separate HEPG2 development set where no query drug expression measurement was supplied, Cell Painting and nonhepatic viability features gave a small kernel gain. On the 345 parents with both views, the relative RMSE improvement against the frozen R17 reference was 0.200%. On all 1,862 parents with a missing-view fallback, it was 0.046%. Adding the same measured features to the FiLM input did not improve both metrics relative to the shared kernel or its mask and parent-permutation controls. [Phenotypic view ablation](../I5_RESULTS.md)

For 493 parents with same-drug RNA inputs in the five held-cell comparisons, the basal-RNA FiLM residual had RMSE 0.000108 higher than the identity/OOV residual (99% interval 0.000022–0.000197) and pooled R² 0.0366 percentage points lower (−0.0768 to −0.0097). Both residual models had lower RMSE than donor averaging on the absolute response. Cell-by-drug interaction error increased in both models. [Matched basal context comparison](../XB_RESULTS.md)

With only structure and fit-vehicle expression as inputs, nested source-only scalar calibration of a fixed-checkpoint FiLM reduced absolute RMSE by 0.121% relative to its uncalibrated prediction (99% interval 0.013–0.231%). Adding a gene-wise vehicle variance correction to that scalar increased RMSE by 0.0000369 and lowered pooled R² by 0.00735 percentage points on the 493-parent, five-context comparison. All three outer folds had the same direction. The calibrated prediction had higher absolute error than the historically tuned X0 reference. [Structure-only shift calibration](../TA_RESULTS.md)

Exact concentration and parent matching yielded five compounds and 21 physical wells in GSE284321 and three compounds and nine wells in GSE296254. On their corresponding independent L1000 observations, the 6-to-24-hour prediction had parent-macro cosine of 0.247 and 0.220. On the identical 827-gene axis in the respective HepaRG datasets, cosine was 0.024 and −0.017. Direct 24-hour L1000 donors also had near-zero mean cosine in HepaRG. These results estimate how much response is reproducible within the HepaRG assay after position adjustment. [HepaRG residual repeats](../HEPARG_POSITION_REPEAT_RESULTS.md)

## Methods

### Physical units and information available at inference

LINCS samples were indexed by release, cell type, canonical compound parent and RNA plate–well coordinate. We required measured concentrations and validated exact dose–time pairs against original instance and signature-quality metadata for the temporal comparison. The model using structure and the model supplied with actual same-compound expression were evaluated separately. In the matched nonhepatic transfer comparison, each donor cell line contributed its mean fit-vehicle contrast, and the four cell lines contributed equally. For validation and scoring, independent vehicle wells formed the reference contrast. Physical wells were kept together across reuse of the same well and rotations of the three vehicle roles. Data reserved for the separately registered confirmation study were excluded from development inputs. [Temporal protocol](../T4_PROTOCOL.md) · [Matched donor protocol](../SL_B_PROTOCOL.md)

### Adult HCC donor transfer

We selected well-level CMap 2020 measurements at exactly 10 µM and 24 hours with published instance QC and a resolved compound structure. The JHH-5 and JHH-7 target wells were part of a prior project development inventory. Source measurements were limited to annotated nonhepatic cell lines, with at least three eligible vehicle wells per cell–RNA plate context. Within each vehicle rotation, source responses were calculated against the fit vehicle group. We averaged treated wells within each source cell and RNA plate, then gave equal weight to plates within a cell and to distinct cells within a chemical parent. Targets used a disjoint vehicle group. We excluded target drug labels, physical plates and Bemis–Murcko scaffolds from comparator fitting. For the JHH-7 comparison, all comparator fitting labels came from JHH-5. [JHH-5 contract](../HCC_LINCS_24H_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md) · [JHH-7 contract](../JHH7_DONOR_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md)

The fit-free estimator attenuated each donor mean using its squared value divided by the sum of its squared value and the cross-cell sample variance divided by the number of donor cells. We assigned zero when that denominator was zero. Cross-cell variance describes disagreement among measured contexts. In the panel-size comparison we fixed eight nested hash-based orders of eligible donors for every parent. All target wells and vehicle rotations of a parent received the same orders. We evaluated each panel's prediction against the same outcome and averaged the resulting losses. [Shrinkage and attribution](../JHH5_DONOR_ATTRIBUTION_PROTOCOL.md) · [Donor subsampling](../HCC_DONOR_PANEL_SIZE_PROTOCOL.md)

In the matched eight-donor comparison, the fixed State Transition architecture took the corresponding donor mean as a single RNA token. The training schedule cycled through the eight panels for each fitting parent, with five visits to each panel in 40 epochs. The origin scalar was fitted on the same JHH-5 training contrasts using all eight panel inputs at equal weight. All comparator predictions were frozen before score-vehicle and target measurements were read. [Matched eight-donor contract](../HCC_DONOR8_STATE_PROTOCOL.md)

We registered a separate exact-dose comparison at 3.33333 µM using the same models, panel draws, gene axis and scoring rules. Its 495 JHH-5 treatment wells had no overlap with the previously evaluated treatment-well IDs. The evaluation shared five RNA plates, all 65 eligible target vehicle wells and 456 compound parents with the 10 µM comparison. Within the low-dose analysis, the scoring plate, parent fold and its scaffolds were excluded from the fit, and donor RNA came from other plates and nonhepatic cell lines. [Low-dose support and prior exposure](../JHH_LOW_DOSE_SUPPORT.md) · [Low-dose contract](../JHH_LOW_DOSE_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md)

A separate dose-bridge comparison predicted 1.11111 µM JHH-5 responses from nonhepatic measurements of the same compound at 1.11111 and 10 µM. Each source context contained both doses for the same Broad compound identifier, cell line and RNA plate, with shared eligible vehicle wells. The target comprised 472 previously unscored treatment wells from 467 compounds. The prediction used two constrained, gene-independent coefficients fitted under the same held parent, scaffold and plate exclusions. Each source alone had a fitted scalar comparator, and a State adaptation received the two dose-specific vectors as separate tokens. A within-evaluation-pool substitution replaced only the high-dose vector with a response to another compound. [Dose-bridge contract](../JHH_DOSE_BRIDGE_PROTOCOL.md)

For the low-dose transfer to JHH-7, we fitted the scalar and the one-token State adaptation only on JHH-5 at 1.11111 µM. We excluded JHH-7 evaluation parents and scaffolds from both fits. Each evaluation prediction used nonhepatic data matched to its JHH-7 concentration. The JHH-7 1 and 0.1 µM measurements were reserved for scoring and analyzed separately on the single available target RNA plate. The wrong-compound control used the fitted scalar and a donor vector from the same evaluation concentration. [JHH-7 low-dose contract](../JHH7_LOW_DOSE_PROTOCOL.md)

For the position-disjoint JHH-5 analysis at 10 µM, we removed every donor well whose RNA row and column matched any target well for the same compound. We selected eight donor cell lines by a fixed hash order. The eight-token State adaptation received all eight donor vectors, and the calibrated shrinkage and scalar models received the same measurements. Each model was fitted with the same held parent, scaffold and physical plate exclusions. A within-evaluation-pool permutation provided wrong-compound controls with the corresponding fitting gain. [Off-position eight-source contract](../JHH5_OFF_POSITION_PROTOCOL.md)

We transferred the same 10 µM recipe to JHH-7. Both scalar gains and the eight-token State comparator used only JHH-5 labels at 10 µM after excluding the chemical parents and scaffolds of the full registered JHH-7 10 µM cohort. The score subset consisted of JHH-7 parents with eight admissible off-position nonhepatic sources. The JHH-7 outcome plate and the fitting plates were physically disjoint. [Cross-cell off-position contract](../JHH7_OFF_POSITION_PROTOCOL.md)

The four added public models received a pseudobasal vector formed from a JHH-5 fit-role mean vehicle and the fitted donor scalar. Each model also received its registered molecular features. Model weights were initialized afresh, and the same 203 JHH-5 compound groups supplied the target contrasts. We used an internal split of 163 fitting and 40 selection parents and disjoint vehicles to select epochs and residual gains, then refit on the full training set. On JHH-5 the same procedure used 96–148 internal fitting and 24–36 selection parents, with 122–183 parents available for each refit after excluding the score plate, parent fold and scaffolds. The best public reference was selected separately for RMSE and pooled R² in each paired resample. [JHH-5 contract](../JHH5_OFF_POSITION_NATIVE_PROTOCOL.md) · [JHH-7 contract](../JHH7_OFF_POSITION_NATIVE_PROTOCOL.md)

### Temporal model and comparators

The 6-hour vector was centered with the source mean and projected onto principal directions learned only from fitting conditions. A ridge map estimated the 24-hour vector from those coordinates, and the fitting mean was added to the output. Each parent had equal total fitting weight. Rank (4, 16 or 32) and ridge penalty (0.1 or 1) were selected from internally held parents and disjoint vehicle means. The early identity vector, a source-fitted nonnegative scalar and a different-plate 24-hour donor provided comparators. Exact-condition controls retained the same trained operator. For transfer to HepaRG we used the registered one-to-one Entrez–Ensembl panel, preserved the frozen L1000 predictions, and computed cosine against independent plate vehicle subtractions in TempO-Seq. [Time model and scoring contract](../T4_PROTOCOL.md) · [Cross-assay protocol](../T4_HEPARG_PROTOCOL.md)

### Estimands and uncertainty

Physical-well root mean square errors were averaged within parent, then across parents. Pooled R² used the total squared error relative to a zero treatment contrast. Paired intervals were calculated from 10,000 resamples of whole chemical parents and their associated wells and vehicle rotations. Primary comparisons across four development axes used one Holm family of directional paired randomization tests retaining the registered A and B slots. The analysis of the small exact HepaRG cohorts used parent-level cosine and observed ranges. [Primary-test registry and adjustment](../data/dev_family/primary.tsv)

## Discussion

The largest observed gain depends on the availability of a matching early measurement. Separating the early input from the evaluation controls and holding out both parent and target plate made it possible to test a temporal model without adapting to the evaluation plate. Sensitivity to replacing the early response with a dose-matched response of the wrong compound supports the value of the measured input. Under the same-information architecture comparison, structural and objective modifications made smaller changes, including cases in which efficiency improved without practically meaningful reductions in error.

The existing cross-platform comparisons are narrow. Exact chemical and concentration matching substantially reduces support. Differences between HEPG2 and HepaRG and between expression assays occur together. The observations cannot isolate a platform effect from a cell-context effect. HEPG2 is hepatoblastoma-derived, so even the same-assay result does not establish drug-response accuracy in adult hepatocellular carcinoma. A separate evaluation needs independent tumour-cell contexts, randomized physical positions, matched time and exposure, independent vehicle roles and the best available comparator under the same inputs. [HEPG2 lineage evidence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.humpath.2009.07.003)

In the exposed Tahoe SNU-423 development data, six of 203 compounds had a nearest fitted ECFP Tanimoto similarity of at least 0.7 and accounted for 90.8% of the positive squared-error reductions from copying a close analogue. Resampling the existing Bemis–Murcko scaffold components gave a sensitivity interval of −0.64 to 15.37 percentage points for the pooled R² change against the strongest comparator. Both scaffold-weighted endpoints encompassed zero. This analysis retained the frozen parent-split predictions, with performance concentrated among structurally supported compounds. [SNU-423 scaffold sensitivity](../HCC_SCAFFOLD_RESULTS.md)

A separate refit used the same held parents and a fixed 978-gene landmark intersection. Removing their scaffold components from fitting reduced the number of held parents with a close analogue from six to two. Relative to removing an equal number of fitting parents at random, scaffold removal increased RMSE for analogue copying by 0.000552 (99% interval 0.000026 to 0.002171). The change in pooled R² was −4.39 percentage points (99% interval −13.27 to −0.18). The scaffold-purged ECFP ridge had higher RMSE and lower R² than the zero-response reference. [Scaffold-purged refit](../HCC_SCAFFOLD_REFIT_RESULTS.md)

We tested whether same-drug external RNA could help where close structural support was sparse. Nonhepatic GSE92742 measurements supplied 100 parent groups for the 24-hour SNU-423 development comparison. Of the 386 source parent–cell combinations, 382 were measured at 10 µM, while the Tahoe target was measured at 5 µM. The low-rank map did not improve both endpoints over either a scalar using the same RNA or frozen State predictions augmented with the same scalar correction. On the 94 parents with Tanimoto below 0.7, the RMSE differences were +0.00338 (99% interval 0.00090–0.00805) versus the scalar and +0.00333 (0.00071–0.00825) versus the best RNA-augmented State comparison. The assay, cellular context and concentration varied together. [SNU-423 external RNA analysis](../SNU423_DONOR_RESULTS.md)

An exact-entity repeat audit in Tahoe SNU-423 found two parents per dose with observations at different well-position indices on different plates. The 5 µM same-position set contained 50 parents. No target well could be evaluated with both kinds of repeat. These supports constrain what can be inferred about response reproducibility across well positions, even after removal of the shared response in a physical well. [Repeat support and technical comparison](../TAHOE_HCC_REPEAT_RESULTS.md)

We also tested whether training on 0.05 and 0.5 µM alongside 5 µM improved the 5 µM SNU-423 prediction. The ECFP dose kernel reduced RMSE from 0.069958 to 0.069950 on the original 203 held parents (paired change −0.000008, 99% interval −0.000141 to 0.000071). The intervals for both endpoints included zero against the high-dose-only fit, shuffled low-dose labels and the best frozen virtual-cell comparator. The matched-input ChemBERTa cosine kernel had higher error. These results apply to the registered kernel and dose weighting. [Dose-sharing comparison](../HCC_DOSE_SHARING_RESULTS.md)
